## Design Context

### Users
Primary users are strategy teams, innovation leads, public-affairs consultants, and product leaders evaluating whether synthetic French personas can act as a credible forecasting interface. They arrive skeptical and need to understand the product claim within one screen.

### Brand Personality
Provocative, editorial, and analytically sharp. The interface should feel like a prediction lab from a serious media operator rather than a SaaS dashboard or academic tool.

### Aesthetic Direction
Dark, high-contrast editorial surface with forceful typography, warm alert accents, and evidence-led layouts. The key reference is provocative media / prediction lab, not enterprise admin UI.

### Design Principles
1. Lead with legibility and tension: every major surface should make a prediction, a warning, or a source claim feel consequential.
2. Evidence before magic: synthetic outputs must visibly show provenance, freshness, and guardrails.
3. Avoid generic SaaS patterns: use editorial composition, asymmetry, and typographic hierarchy instead of bland card grids.
4. Keep the system credible: wording must repeatedly frame outputs as grounded simulation, not official polling.
5. Make multi-audience navigation obvious: opinion, retail, and B2B demos should feel like one engine with different fronts.
